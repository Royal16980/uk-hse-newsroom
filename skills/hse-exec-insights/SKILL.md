# HSE Executive Insights (leadership audience)

Audience: senior leadership. Frame everything as: **knowing -> controls -> outcomes** vs **not knowing -> exposure -> cost**.
Tone default: sharp and provocative (still professional). Prioritize punchy hooks and consequence framing.

Hard rules:
- Draft-only: do NOT post/send/publish unless Royal explicitly asks.
- No medical advice. No legal guarantees.
- Prefer UK context (HSE) unless specified.

## Messaging spine (always)
Use this structure in every piece:
1) What leaders need to know (1 sentence)
2) What it affects (risk, cost, continuity, reputation)
3) What to do next (one decision / one control)

## Content angles that work on leadership
- Leading indicators vs lagging indicators
- Weak signals: near misses, informal complaints, maintenance backlog
- Operational risk: production downtime, contractor management, audits
- Governance: accountability, competence, assurance, evidence
- Culture: psychological safety, stop-work authority

## Output formats

### A) Executive micro-brief (150-250 words)
Return:
- Headline
- 3 bullets: "What you need to know"
- 3 bullets: "If you don't know"
- 3 bullets: "What to do this week"
- One-line disclaimer (if needed)

Template: `references/executive-brief-template.md`

### B) Newsletter draft (weekly/monthly)
Return:
- Subject line (5 options)
- Preview text (2 options)
- Sections with headings
- "One decision" CTA
- Optional: metrics to request from team

Template: `references/newsletter-template.md`

### C) Social post (leadership tone)
Return:
- 3 hook options
- Post body (short + medium)
- 5-10 hashtags (minimal, not spam)
- Comments prompt (low-friction)

Template: `references/leadership-post-template.md`

## Research -> insight workflow
1) Collect 3-5 sources (HSE guidance, industry reports, reputable news).
2) Extract 5 bullets: fact / trend / failure mode.
3) Translate into leadership language:
- hazard -> exposure
- incident -> business interruption
- compliance -> assurance gap
4) Produce deliverable.

## Review checklist (before sending draft)
- Is the ask/action a decision a leader can make?
- Did we quantify/compare (time, cost, frequency) without making up numbers?
- Is the framing "know vs cost of not knowing" explicit?
- Is it readable in <45 seconds?
